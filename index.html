The door

One gate, and it's for devs.

A launchpad on Raydium LaunchLab. A wallet asks to launch, a person reads what that wallet has done, and the answer is yes or no. Nobody is asked anything to buy.

1

Ask.

Connect, sign a message, and that's the whole form. The wallet's record is the application.

2

Pick your rules.

Pair, tax, where the tax goes, who it pays, how often. Fixed before launch, signed, public.

3

The engine runs it.

Each round the tax is collected, swapped into what you chose, split by your rule and sent. Every round goes up, transactions included.

The choices

Ten decisions. All of them the dev's.

Elsewhere these come baked in. Here each one is set at launch and written into rules the dev signs.

01

The pair

Raise against any quote asset Raydium has a config for: SOL or USDC, gold, tokenized stocks. New ones appear here as Raydium adds them.

02

The tax

Anywhere from 0 to 5%, fixed on the curve. We don't touch a token's rate afterwards, ever.

03

Where the tax goes

Back into the pair, into any token Jupiter can route, into the token itself, into a burn, into a treasury. Any mix of those.

04

Who it pays

Evenly, by time held, to whoever never sold, or by draw. Ten wallets earn what one would have.

05

The buy-tax refund

Switch it on and each round hands buyers their tax back, matched to the buy it came from. Sellers pay.

06

A second rule set

A different split that kicks in once the token graduates, or once holders or market cap reach a number you set. Signed up front, no way back.

07

The wait

A fresh buy earns nothing for 1 hour to 7 days, your pick. Sell before then and that share lands with the wallets that waited.

08

The jackpot

Let a slice build up and draw it every N rounds; pot size and countdown sit on the token page.

09

The team lock

A slice Raydium's program locks at launch: nothing until the cliff, then a straight-line release after graduation.

10

The referral cut

Whoever brings a buyer can get as much as 1% of that curve trade, paid by the program as part of the trade. The number is the dev's.

Not optional

Same for every token here.

Sell as the dev and your slice goes to the holders. Permanently.

The launch wallet and the wallets you named are read every round. Any of them holding less than last time, and the creator and treasury portions of the tax go to holders from that round on. Buying it back changes nothing.

One ticker, one token, thirty days.

Case doesn't matter and neither does a $ in front. After thirty days it's open again.

Nobody sees the mint early.

The launch transaction travels as a private bundle; the mint is unseen until its block lands. Inside that block, bots can still buy.

Leftovers are never ours.

When a share is too tiny to send, it is shared out among the holders who were paid that round, or it carries over to the next one. Nothing rounds off into the platform.

Claimed daily, forwarded, shown per token. The figure doesn't move; the platform's own slice absorbs whatever Raydium changes.

The pool-fee line on each token page.

Every round is published and can be re-run.

Balances, the rule, who got what, the lottery seed, the signatures. The token page re-runs the round from its own snapshot and says whether it matched.

Graduated liquidity can't leave.

At graduation the LP splits: one part locked, one part burned, both staying in the pool for good. The mint authority is dropped when the token is created.

On-chain, at every graduation.

Only approved wallets get past the launch step.

The app turns an unapproved wallet away right there, and the token list shows launches made through it and nothing else. Being approved means one thing: this wallet was allowed to launch. It covers nothing after that.
